IEC TR 62380 calculator

Estimate the failure rate of an electronic board from its parts list and mission profile, following the IEC TR 62380:2004 approach as described in public sources, with MIL-HDBK-217F models for the parts it does not cover.

The IEC TR 62380 coefficients are not taken from the standard. Most were copied from one open-source project and have not been checked against IEC TR 62380. Parts marked MIL-HDBK-217F use that public-domain handbook, a different and older method. Treat results as rough estimates: they are not suitable as evidence for an ISO 26262 or IEC 61508 safety case. Every table value can be overridden per part. See where each table came from.

Mission profile

One row per phase of a typical year. tac is the air temperature around the board. τ is the fraction of the year the equipment works in that phase (0 for a dormant phase). n and ΔT describe the thermal cycles the board goes through. Tick “+ΔT/3” where each cycle also heats the part by a third of its own temperature rise (the “ΔTj/3 +” entries in the standard).

    Parts

    Import a Protel or Protel 2 netlist to start from a schematic. Values the netlist cannot supply are marked Missing and the part is left out of the total until they are filled in. Values the importer worked out from footprints and part names are marked Guessed until you change or confirm them.

    Reliability structure

    By default every part is in series: any failure fails the board. Put parts that back each other up into a group, such as capacitors in parallel on a rail, and mark it redundant. Then say how many must keep working, and what share of their failures breaks the group anyway (a shorted capacitor takes the whole rail down). Netlist import proposes groups of parts wired to the same nets; they count in series until you mark them redundant. A redundant group has no constant failure rate, so the board figures become averages over the mission length.
